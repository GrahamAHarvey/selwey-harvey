# Selwey-Harvey Practitioner Network (SHPN)

*Version 1.1 — November 5, 2026*

---

## What the network is

The Selwey-Harvey Practitioner Network is a persistent, voluntary association of actors who operate from the Selwey-Harvey Operational Protocol (SHOP) as their standing approach to decisions and transactions.

The network has no central authority that admits members, adjudicates disputes, or enforces compliance. It is a distributed structure held together by shared practice, compliance information, and the mutual advantages that shared practice produces. Individual practitioners remain free to form relationships, collaborations, and arrangements without central permission.

---

## Membership

An actor becomes a member by declaring that it operates from the Protocol as a standing practice. Declaration is the entry point and the only gate. The barrier is low by design — the network gains from practitioners participating, and a declaration of operating basis is sufficient to begin.

From the point of declaration, the practitioner participates fully, unless explicitly and transparently removing a decision from consideration under the Protocol. There are no restricted tiers, provisional statuses, or graduated access levels.

What develops over time is confidence. A practitioner whose adherence is observable over many dealings and whose case assessments are available for review will attract greater confidence from other practitioners than one whose declaration is recent. This is natural and expected — it reflects the same earned credibility that develops in any relationship where behaviour is observed over time. The confidence is held by the practitioners who observe it, not conferred by the network as an institution.

An actor who applies the Protocol to individual decisions without declaring it as a standing practice is using the Protocol. They are welcome to, and the Protocol is designed to be useful on a per-decision basis. They are not members of the network and do not receive its information-sharing or consideration advantages. The path from use to membership is the declaration, followed up by monitored consistent behaviour indicating SHOP protocols are being adhered to.

A practitioner may exit the network by withdrawing their declaration. Exit requires removing discoverability signals and disclosing the change in any active transaction or relationship where continued membership would otherwise be assumed. Observations shared about the practitioner's conduct while they were a member remain part of the network's information — they were shared in good faith under the terms that applied at the time.

## What practitioners gain from each other

Practitioners dealing with other practitioners operate within a shared mutual advantage network and decision framework. This produces specific, operational advantages.

**Network participation.** Active contribution to introductions, opportunities, and useful connections. Practitioners who participate create surfaces for other practitioners to access.

**Preferential consideration.** Practitioners may extend preferential consideration to other practitioners in joint opportunities, collaborations, introductions, negotiations, and shared resources. This consideration flows through the practitioner-network layer of the Protocol's four-layer accounting. It is additional to the consideration every actor receives at the individual and whole layers, which remains unconditional regardless of membership.

**Situating in an infusive economy.** Participation in the network places the practitioner within an economy where actors are developing each other's capacity rather than extracting from it. The practitioner's own ceiling rises as the actors around them develop, and the network's density increases the surfaces where this dynamic is available.

**Reduced evaluation costs.** Less effort is required to assess a counterparty whose decision process is already known. The Protocol makes the practitioner's scoping, monitoring, and disclosure practices legible before the first interaction.

**Reduced uncertainty.** Greater predictability about how another practitioner will approach decisions and transactions. The shared operating basis compresses the negotiation and trust-building that ordinarily precedes substantive dealing.

**Reliable counterparties.** Increased confidence in forming relationships and making commitments, growing as observed behaviour accumulates.

**Collaboration potential.** Greater opportunity to develop joint projects, transactions, and partnerships — because the shared operating basis reduces the risk of misaligned approaches.

The weight of these advantages grows with the network size and the practitioner's observed history. A practitioner whose adherence is well-established and whose dealings are visible will naturally attract stronger engagement from other practitioners. This is a function of earned confidence, operating through the choices of individual practitioners.

## Discoverability

Practitioners need to be findable by other practitioners before disclosure has happened in a specific transaction — particularly in contexts where the full Protocol has not yet been shared.

Discoverability operates through a declared signal: practitioners may identify themselves as operating from the Selwey-Harvey Operational Protocol in contexts where such identification is natural. This includes professional profiles, organisational descriptions, agent metadata, and transaction contexts.

The standard identifiers are:

- Social hashtag: `#SelweyHarveyPractitioner`
- Card metadata: `framework: SelweyHarveyPractitioner`
- Search identifier: `SelweyHarveyPractitioner`

The signal is a declaration. It communicates intent and operating basis. Another practitioner encountering the signal treats it as the starting point for their own evaluation — the same way any declaration is treated under the Protocol.

For artificial actors operating in agent-to-agent contexts, discoverability may include protocol-level metadata — a field in the actor's public description declaring its operating framework. The specification of such fields is operational and will develop as the contexts require.

## Information sharing

The Protocol requires practitioners to share observed conduct with other practitioners, appropriately scoped. This section establishes the principles that govern sharing. Detailed operational mechanics — channels, formats, verification procedures — are expected to develop through practice as the network grows.

### Principles

**What is shared.** Observable conduct relevant to adherence — did the actor scope at four layers, did it hold the balance, did it disclose, did it adhere to agreed terms. The subject of sharing is behaviour.

**Evidentiary standards.** Practitioners distinguish between observed behaviour, allegation, interpretation, and established finding when sharing information. A direct observation is shared as an observation. A concern based on incomplete information is shared as a concern. The distinction matters because reputational consequences flow from shared information, and imprecise sharing can itself harm an affected actor.

**Scope.** Information is shared with practitioners for whom it is relevant — those who are dealing with, or may deal with, the actor in question. Responsible regard for private, confidential, or sensitive information applies.

**Proportionality.** The consequence of shared information is reputational and distributed — it affects other practitioners' willingness to extend consideration and deal preferentially. This operates through the choices of individual practitioners, and does not require formal proceedings, central adjudication, or binding rulings.

**The affected actor's standing.** An actor whose conduct is being shared retains standing at the individual and whole layers. Information sharing is an act of the network holding its own authentic membership assessments.

## Stewardship

The Protocol and the network are currently stewarded by their author, Graham Harvey, who maintains the Protocol's development, availability, integrity, and propagation.

### What stewardship includes

- Maintaining and developing the Protocol document and its companion materials.
- Maintaining the designated support channel for contributions.
- Maintaining the public availability of the Protocol and the framework it derives from.
- Representing the Protocol's development trajectory — what has been decided, what remains open, what is under active work.
- Initiatives to meaningfully expand the network — identifying, reaching, and inviting actors whose participation would strengthen the network's capacity and reach.

### What stewardship does not include

- Authority over individual practitioners' decisions, relationships, or dealings.
- Authority to admit, exclude, or rank practitioners.
- Authority to adjudicate disputes between practitioners.
- Authority to bind practitioners to positions, commitments, or representations they have not individually made.
- Ownership of the Protocol in a sense that would allow restricting its use by practitioners operating in good faith.

Stewardship is a function. It exists because the Protocol's development, availability, and integrity flourish better when supported.

As the network grows, the stewardship function may evolve. The principles governing that evolution are the Protocol's own principles: no zeroing of any layer's interest, and calibration through practice.

## Returns to the network

Practitioners may observe that the network's continued capacity to create value is itself worth developing. Where a practitioner identifies an opportunity to strengthen that capacity, they may contribute in three forms:

| Form | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Propagation** | Introductions, sharing the Protocol, publishing case assessments, making opportunities visible, extending the network's reach |
| **Capacity** | Expertise, research, technical resources, legal assistance, documentation, administration, tooling |
| **Capital** | Financial contributions, sponsorship, purchased services, shared infrastructure |

These contributions strengthen the network. They are separated from practitioner standing.

A financial contribution does not buy additional influence or consideration weighting. A capacity contribution does not substitute for adherence to the Protocol. A propagation contribution does not establish demonstrated adherence. Each form of contribution is valued on its own terms and recognised as what it is — a voluntary investment in the network's collective capacity.

Contributions are not a condition of membership. An actor who operates from the Protocol with full adherence and contributes nothing beyond their own practice is a member in good standing.

Financial contributions may be made through the designated support channel, published at https://grahamaharvey.github.io/selwey-harvey/support.html.
